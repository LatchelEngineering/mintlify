<Note>
  This job aid walks a PS specialist through working a Deep Dive (DD) ticket from pick-up to close. For the big picture (DD types, SLAs, and how requests come in), see [Product Support Deep Dive Management](/latchel-admin/Product-Support-Team/Product-Support-Deep-Dive-Management). For how requests are submitted, see [How to: Submit a Deep Dive Request](/latchel-admin/Product-Support-Team/How-to-Submit-a-Deep-Dive-Request).
</Note>

## **Step-by-Step Guide**

| Step | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **(1) Pick up** | Review the request, start the timer, assign yourself, label, and categorize |
| **(2) Investigate** | Build the timeline using every tool |
| **(3) Document gaps** | Create child issue tickets or Reported Issue Found notes |
| **(4) Conclude** | Write the internal conclusion and tag the root cause |
| **(5) Workshop** | Submit the customer communication for review in `#ps-team-chat` |
| **(6) Close** | Post the conclusion, tag the requester, stop the timer |

<Warning>
  **Invoice disputes are handled differently.** If the ticket came from the **Invoice Dispute DD** workflow, follow [SOP: Handling Work Order Invoice Disputes](/latchel-admin/Product-Support-Team/SOP-Handling-Work-Order-Invoice-Disputes) for eligibility, refunds, and outcomes. The investigation steps below still apply.
</Warning>

### **(1) Receive the Submission & Review the Complaint**

When a DD request comes in, read it carefully so you understand:

- The problem being raised
- **The one question the DD needs to answer** (from the "What question should the DD answer?" field)
- What the requester already checked, and what the customer expected to happen
- Any prior commitments already made to the customer

**Best Practice:** Always read the **title and description** of the ticket first to get an overview of the dive you're about to conduct. If the question isn't clear, ask the requester in the Slack thread before you start.

<Note>
  **Visibility:** Keep the **Team Owner and Viewer** set to `PS Deep Dive Tickets` so the whole PS team can view and collaborate.
</Note>

#### **When reviewing a Deep Dive ticket, follow these steps:**

1. In the Wavelength (WL) ticket, go to the **Attributes** section (right panel) and click the **START** button on the timer when you begin reviewing.
   - Use the same button to **pause** the timer during breaks or other work.

<Warning>
  **Why it matters:**

  - Timer tracking powers our **Handling Time** metric.
  - Our goal is a working time within **4 hours** per deep dive (most take 1–3 hours).
</Warning>

<Frame>
  ![image-20250925-183153.png](/images/PST-79.png)
</Frame>

2. Assign yourself as the **Owner** of the ticket.
3. Under **Labels**, check the box to categorize it as either **DD (Deep Dive)** or **SDDI**.

<Frame>
  ![image-20250925-183438.png](/images/PST-80.png)
</Frame>

4. To select the **Complaint Category**, click the **\+ icon** and search for the right category.
   - Follow the [Job Aid: PS DD and SDDI Category Matrix](/latchel-admin/Product-Support-Team/Job-Aid-PS-DD-and-SDDI-Category-Matrix).

<Frame>
  ![image-20250925-183646.png](/images/PST-81.png)
</Frame>

5. Keep the ticket in:
   - **Needs Response**: new submissions not yet picked up (backlog).
   - **Open**: tickets you're actively working on.

<Warning>
  **Why it matters:**

  - DDs have a **3-day SLA**.
  - If a ticket stays in **Needs Response** and approaches the 3-day mark, **WL flags it as Breaching**.
  - This is how we track our **Completion Time** metric.
</Warning>

### **(2) Use Resources & Build the Timeline**

Start by **gathering everything** related to the issue: support tickets, customer communications, system logs, and any other source that shows what happened. The goal is a **complete and accurate picture** before you analyze it.

The type of complaint decides **what information you need**, **which tools you use**, and **how you approach it**. The starting point is always the same: a clear **timeline or high-level overview** of what occurred.

---

#### **Timeline Creation**

- **Start from the beginning** of the event and work forward in order.
- **Document each key action**: what happened, when, and who was involved.
- **Note the outcome** of each action and how it changed the state of the work order.
- **Highlight gaps or inconsistencies** that may point to where the issue started.

<Warning>
  **Why this matters:** A well-structured timeline makes it easier to pinpoint when and where the breakdown happened, which removes guesswork and speeds up resolution.
</Warning>

<Note>
  **Time Tracking:** Track the time you spend on each part of the investigation (timeline, child issues, and conclusion) in the WL ticket as **internal notes**. This helps us keep both **quality** and **efficiency** consistent across deep dives.
</Note>

Don't just copy events. **Interpret and connect** them. Understand _why_ each action was taken and how it affected the next step. That turns a list of actions into an investigative tool that gets you to the root cause faster.

---

#### **Building the Timeline**

1. **Gather Information**
   - Collect every source related to the issue:
     1. Related **work orders** and WL tickets
     2. **Customer communications** (emails, SMS, calls, webchat transcripts)
     3. **Internal threads** (Slack posts, escalation notes)
     4. **System activity logs** (settings updates, task creation, dispatch events, action sets, automation triggers)
     5. Any **attachments or evidence** (photos, estimates, invoices, vendor notes, access instructions)

<Note>
  **Using AI:** You can use Claude to summarize long work order logs and draft a first-pass timeline, but AI output is an **input, not a finding**.

  - **Verify every key fact** (dates, times, amounts, who did what) against the original source before it goes in the timeline.
  - AI can't listen to call recordings or confirm what a photo shows. Review those yourself.
  - For Latchel process questions, use the **Mintlify assistant** in the Help Center, not Rovo (Rovo still searches the outdated Confluence SOPs).
</Note>

---

2. **Include These Details in Each Timeline Entry**
   - At minimum, capture:
     - **Date and Time:** When the action occurred
     - **Action Taken:** What happened (e.g., task created, SP sourced, SMS sent, severity updated)
     - **User:** Who performed the action (PM, Associate, SP, Resident, System)
     - **Tool/Link:** Where the action took place (WO, Slack thread, ticket, etc.)
     - **Evidence:** Screenshots, messages, photos, or other relevant details

<Note>
  Keep entries short, structured, and consistent. Focus on actions and results, not opinions or assumptions.
</Note>

---

3. **Create Entries**

Once key events are identified, **build the timeline in chronological order**. Each entry should be clear enough that another specialist, or any department, could follow it without more explanation.

---

<Check>
  For **SDDI tickets**, **skip the full timeline.** Give a **short, high-level summary** of what happened, focused only on the specific complaint.

  - 3–5 bullet points are enough.
  - Keep it short, objective, and tied to the resident's or PM's feedback.
</Check>

#### **Tips for Creating an Exceptional Timeline**

- **Be Clear & Concise:** Use short, factual statements.
- **Stay Objective:** Avoid assumptions or blame in the timeline itself.
- **Attach Evidence:** Support entries with screenshots, links, or files where needed.
- **Maintain Chronology:** List events in the exact order they occurred.
- **Make It Skimmable:** Another specialist should be able to understand it in minutes.

---

### **(3) Identify Gaps & Create Feedback**

A core goal of every DD is to identify, document, and communicate **any gaps, errors, or improvement opportunities** you find. This includes both **core issues tied to the complaint** and **secondary oversights** that could cause future failures.

Capturing and escalating these findings helps us:

- Prevent similar issues from recurring
- Strengthen internal processes and accountability
- Reduce future complaints

---

#### **Documenting Issues: Child Issue Tickets vs. Reported Issue Found Note**

- **Reported Issue Found Note**: use when the cause involves **external stakeholders or account setup**, not internal teams. Common examples:
  - PM or resident errors or misunderstandings
  - LCN / Preferred Vendor issues
- **Child Issue Tickets**: use when the gap involves **Latchel teams, systems, or processes**. Child tickets formally document and route inappropriate actions, missed steps, system behavior, or process gaps **directly tied to the complaint**, so the right team can:
  - Address associate, support, or vendor performance issues
  - Review Latchel Vendor Network (LVN) performance
  - Fix product/platform behavior
  - Correct account settings or configuration
  - Review and implement process improvements

---

### **(4) Conclude**

The **conclusion** explains the findings, decisions, and preventive actions from the investigation. It should **answer the question the DD was opened for**.

#### **Key Components of a Strong Internal Conclusion**

1. **What Happened?**

- A clear, concise summary of the original complaint and key findings.
- Check that the conclusion answers the question in the original request.
- The critical events from the timeline that support your findings.

2. **Why Did It Happen?**

- The **root cause** and contributing factors.
- Plain language, based on facts rather than assumptions.
- The supporting evidence (timeline entries, communication logs, process gaps).

3. **What Happens Next? (Resolution & Prevention)**

- The **resolution** for the customer and how their concern is addressed.
- The **preventive actions** or process improvements (including child tickets) to reduce the risk of recurrence.
- **Your recommendation** for the best path forward and why, in your own words.

<Warning>
  **Don't loop back to the SP for "more detail" when the case is already resolvable.** If the evidence already answers the question (for example, the invoice was approved, the estimate went to the PM, and the line items are there), conclude. Only go back to an SP when something is genuinely missing, and say exactly what you need and why.
</Warning>

### **(5) Workshop the Customer Communication**

The investigation is our internal process; the customer email is the customer's experience. **Before anything goes to the customer**, get the communication reviewed:

1. Draft the customer communication using the [DD Customer Conclusion Email Framework (ACE)](/latchel-admin/Product-Support-Team/DD-Customer-Conclusion-Email-Framework-ACE):
   - **A**cknowledge the impact (what the customer experienced, not "we reviewed").
   - **C**larify the cause in plain English (no timeline dumps).
   - **E**xecute the resolution: a concrete action, a named owner, and a timeframe. End with a statement, not a question.
2. Submit the draft in `#ps-team-chat` using the **DD Conclusion Workshop** workflow.
3. Wait for the PS Manager's review, make any changes, and only then send it (or hand it to the requester to send).

<Note>
  **Effort is not communication.** A detailed investigation summary that leaves the customer without a clear answer is a communication failure, no matter how much work went into it.
</Note>

---

### **(6) Close the Deep Dive Ticket**

Closing the ticket properly makes sure the investigation is documented, the requester is notified, and the data is captured for reporting.

##### **With a WL Ticket**

1. **Post the conclusion** as an **internal note** on the DD WL ticket, including:
   - The **conclusion** (what happened, why, what's next)
   - The **DD ticket link**
   - The **Work Order (WO) link**
2. **Tag the requester** (the person who submitted the DD request) so they see it.
3. **Update the ticket status** to **Escalated** to show the investigation is complete and documented.
4. **Stop the timer** and close the DD WL ticket.

#### **Without a WL Ticket**

If the DD was submitted only through Slack:

1. **Post the conclusion** as a reply in the `#ps-dd-sddi` thread where the request was submitted.
2. Include all **relevant links** (work order, related tickets) and a **clear, structured summary**.
3. Tag the requester to let them know the DD is complete.
4. Update any relevant internal trackers if required.
