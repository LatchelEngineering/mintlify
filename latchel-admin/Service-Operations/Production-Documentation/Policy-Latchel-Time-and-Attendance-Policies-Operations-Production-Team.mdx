# 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

The purpose of this policy is to outline Latchel’s time and attendance expectations for Operations Production Team employees and contractors. It ensures consistent standards for tracking attendance, promotes accountability, and provides clear guidelines to support fair performance evaluation.

<Note>
**Who should read this document?**

Latchel Employees and Contractors (Hourly and Salaried Independent Contractors / Freelancers) **working on day-to-day operations (production),** such as associates, operations support specialists, coaches, trainers, specialists, etc.

Ops Leadership should also read this policy to be informed on how this is applicable to their team. Ops Supervisors and Managers are not required to punch in and out.
</Note>

<Note>
**Training Content**

The contents of this page have been included in the **Latchel Time and Attendance Policies** and **Clock In & Out and Time Off** Coassemble courses.
</Note>

# Content

## **Attendance Communication**

If you have an emergency, please let your supervisor and Operations Support Specialists know via Slack when you will be absent and when you will return to work.

When you are back online, follow the time-off process to submit your request for paid or unpaid time to cover the time you have missed.

Please communicate your absence as soon as you are aware of it, so we can adjust our team to handle the work. We know emergencies happen, but a lack of communication in your absence is unacceptable. It impacts our ability to support our customers and also impacts your peers. Be customer-obsessed and a team player.

\*\*Operations Support Specialists, as you get the absence message, please post it in the operations\_support channel, so leadership is also aware.

## **Clock-in Expectation**

The Operations Production team is expected to be logged in and ready to work at the beginning of their schedule. If you need time to boot up and set up your computer, this needs to be done in advance of the start of your schedule. This is similar to “commuting” to your work. You plan to leave your house and be there on time. When working from home, your “commuting” time is your set-up time.

Latchel is a 100% remote work company that relies heavily on the team members having a quiet, distraction-free work environment, a stable electric power source, a computer with sufficient processing power to handle multiple tools, as well as a stable high-speed internet connection. This is a requirement to work in the company, but Latchel understands that there are situations out of the control of the team member.

When the team member is dealing with any issue that is impacting their ability to fully perform all functions of their job, the team member MUST communicate the limitation to your direct Manager, Supervisor, or Operations Support Specialists, who will decide if the team member should:

**(1)** log out until the situation is resolved and request time off;

**(2)** remain working in a limited capacity, such as working only on emails or SMS. The decision is solely at the discretion of Ops Leadership and will be made based on business needs.

## **Time Off Policy**

**Applicable to**: Hourly workers and Salaried workers working on day-to-day operations

Time off should be requested in advance to allow our team to assess operational needs and adjust accordingly. Latchel requires a minimum of **5 days in advance** for all team members to notify Latchel of their absences. A Request is not approved until your direct supervisor has approved it. Requests may be subject to refusal if the company doesn’t have adequate coverage for your absence. The approval or denial should be communicated to the requester within 48 hours of the request submission.

We know that unexpected situations happen, and we provide some flexibility for those exception scenarios. Although Latchel provides this flexibility, the use of this process should be minimized. Excessive unexpected absences impact the performance of the overall team, and Latchel reserves the right to address those in a case-by-case scenario.

“Unexpected” situations include time missed due to any type of outage (internet, power, computer issues, etc), or an unplanned situation that caused/will cause you to miss work that you were unable to report within the 5 days required. This includes bereavement leave (i.e. time off when there is a death or funeral of a member of the employee's immediate family), sick leave (i.e. time off due to health issues), or any other emergency situation.

###   
When an unexpected situation occurs, please notify us immediately in the #status\_updates Slack channel.  
Any time missed, regardless of duration, should be reported using the Temporarily Unavailable Workflow.  
If the time missed is 10 minutes or more, Ops Supervisors will reconcile the Deputy timesheets based on these Temporarily Unavailable submissions.

  
**Planned Time Off** or **Unexpected Time Missed** can be requested to be paid or unpaid. (Subject to Supervisor approval and discretion.) Unpaid Time-Off (UPTO) will be limited to a maximum of 2 weeks, and extended/long leaves will require supervisor approval\*. Please review the **“Paid Time Off” policy** to understand what is applicable to you. Upon management approval, Latchel may offer an option to compensate for hours not worked. See the **“Time Compensation” policy** for more information.

  
**Process documentation:**

-   **How to submit Planned Time Off →** Go to Deputy > Click on your profile, located in the top right corner > Select **“Profile”**\> Leave > Add New. You will be able to see your PTO balance. You will be able to select either Paid Time Off (PTO) or Unpaid Time Off (UPTO, reflected as “Unpaid Leave” in Deputy) 
    
-   **How to report Unexpected Time Off  →** Go to the #status\_updates Slack Channel, click on the “lighting bolt icon” next to the message box and select the “Temporarily Unavailable” form.
    
-   **How to request to work at a limited capacity** →  Reach out to your manager or Operations Support Specialists for approval.
    

  
\*Note: Latchel is not eligible for the FMLA act, which requires to employ 50 or more employees within 75 miles. See more details here: [https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/benefits-leave/fmla](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/benefits-leave/fmla)

## **Paid Time Off Policy**

**Applicable to**: Hourly workers and Salaried workers

Latchel offers up to 120 hours of paid time off for full-time workers per year, accrued biweekly.  There is a max of 160 hours that can be accrued. PTO is available after 90 days of contract. PTO is accrued at 0.0846 **per hour worked.** Therefore, if a team member is on any type of unpaid leave, PTO does **not** accrue for those hours. PTO will carry over to the next calendar year if not used; however, it will not be eligible to be cashed out if not taken or paid upon termination of employment.

Everyone eligible for PTO has a floating holiday (extra 8 hours). Floating holidays are available at the beginning of each calendar year for all who are eligible. However, the floating holiday should only be used for religious or cultural holidays and employee birthdays.  
Team members must specify the event for which they are requesting to use a floating holiday. The request must be scheduled and approved in advance by the employee's immediate supervisor. The approval or denial should be communicated to the requester within 48 hours of the request submission.  
Floating holidays will not be carried over to the next calendar year, nor may they be cashed out if not taken or paid upon termination of employment.

## **Sick (Personal/Carer's) Leave**

Operations are entitled to 7 days of Sick Leave per year, which accrues at 2.1536 hours per bi-weekly pay period if 80 hours are worked. Sick Leave can be used for personal illness or to care for a sick family member. If fewer than 80 hours are worked, accrual will be adjusted accordingly.

For absences longer than 2 days, if sick leave is insufficient, upon supervisor’s discretion - supervisors may allow the use of PTO, but documentation such as a doctor’s note or any verification for absences is required. Latchel encourages using Sick Leave for mental health or rest days should you not been able to use them, provided requests are made 5 days in advance. If all Sick Leave is exhausted without proper documentation, Unpaid Leave will be applied.

## **Meals and Breaks Policy**

**Applicable to**: Hourly workers and Salaried workers working on day-to-day operations

Latchel will apply US law requirements for meals and breaks for all non-exempt (hourly) team members: “_The federal rule does not require an employer to provide either a meal period or breaks. However, if an employer chooses to do so, breaks, usually of the type lasting less than twenty minutes, must be paid. Meal or lunch periods (typically thirty minutes or more) do not need to be paid, so long as the employee is completely relieved of all duties and free to do as they wish during the meal or lunch period._” 

In addition to Federal Laws, each US state may have additional requirements. Latchel has defined this policy based on California and Washington laws which covers most of the states. The general rule is that: (1) the team member will take a 15-minute paid break after working 3 consecutive hours. If the team member works 5 hours, the team member should take a 30-minute unpaid break. Breaks  **CANNOT**, under any circumstances, be combined and taken together, nor can they be taken together with their unpaid 30-minute lunchtime.

Meals and Breaks Guideline:

-   For an **8hrs shif**t, the team member will take their unpaid 30min lunch at the middle of the shift (at the beginning of the 5th hour, and no later than the end of the 5th hour),  and will take a 15 min paid break in the middle of the first half of the shift, and in the middle of the second half of the shift. The paid break needs to be taken, the latest, at the end of 3hrs of consecutive work. 
    
-   For a **10hrs shift,** the team member will take their unpaid 30min lunch at the middle of the shift (at the beginning of the 6th hour, and no later than the end of the 6th hour), and will take a 15 min paid break in the middle of the first half of the shift, and in the middle of the second half of the shift. The paid break needs to be taken the latest at the end of 3hrs of consecutive work.
    
-   For a **12hrs shift**, the team member will take their first unpaid 30min lunch at the beginning of the 6th hour, and their second 30min unpaid break at the beginning of their 10th hour.  During the 1st five hours segment of the shift, the team member will take a 15min paid break in the middle of that segment. The same will apply to the 2nd five hours segment of the shift. Then, the team member will return for the last 2hrs of work with no additional break. If working less than 12hrs, the team member can waive their second 30 min unpaid lunch., but they will need to take a 15min paid break. Leadership will need to also approve exchanging the 30min unpaid lunch with the 15min paid break. **Ops Managers - this is the max number of hours you can have a team member working without further approval.**
    
-   **For a shift of 13hrs or 14hrs**, the team member will follow the same policy as the 12hrs hours shift, however, the 2nd 30min unpaid break must be taken and they will have another 15min paid break when reaching the 12hrs mark. **Ops Managers – this should require approval of COO.**
    

**Note**: (1) Meals and Breaks will need to be adjusted if the employee resides in New York. In that case, consult [https://dol.ny.gov/](https://dol.ny.gov/). (2) For a summary of each state law, visit: [https://www.deputy.com/blog/meal-and-rest-break-laws-by-state](https://www.deputy.com/blog/meal-and-rest-break-laws-by-state) 

## **Time Compensation Policy**

**Applicable to**: Hourly workers working on day-to-day operations

Latchel offers an opportunity to team members to compensate for missed work time to avoid payment deductions. The use of this policy should be minimized and requires Ops Leadership approval. The decision to approve or deny this request is solely at the discretion of Ops Leadership and will be made based on business needs.

While defining a compensation plan, Ops Leadership must ensure that the compensation plan will not result in overtime payment (See **Overtime Policy**) and that the team member will NOT have a shift longer than 12hrs. Any compensation time needs to happen within the **SAME WEEK** the hours were missed.

  

**Compensation Time Requirements** 

-   Compensation time must be requested at the time of reporting time-off (planned or unexpected)
    
-   Compensation time needs to be added to either the beginning or end of the shift; i.e. we will not approve to compensate time during break and lunch times
    
-   We will not approve compensation time that will extend the shift over 12 hrs
    
-   We will not approve compensation time that will result in overtime payment
    
-   Will not be approved outside of business hours - not before 6:30 am PST, not after 7:00 pm PST.
    

**Compensation options:** 

-   **Missed Full Shift:**
    
    -   **Option 1:** Work a full shift another day, i.e. work on a day you have off in your current schedule,. However, you cannot work 7 consecutive days.
        
    -   **Option 2:**  Spread hours across a few days. For example, work extra 2hrs/day. In this case, the Ops Supervisor must adjust the meal and break schedule based on the **“Meal and Break” Policy**
        
-   **Missed Partial shift**
    
    -   **Option 1:** Compensate hours missed in a day that you usually have off in your current schedule
        
    -   **Option 2:** Spread hours across a few days. In this case, the Ops Supervisor must adjust the meal and break schedule based on the **“Meal and Break” Policy**
        

**Process documentation:**

**How to request time compensation? Time compensation should be included in the Time Off Request. However, if you were not planning to compensate for the time, but later decided to request that, you need to submit a “Makeup time request” to your Manager.  Your manager will update your shift in Deputy to reflect that.**

## **Overtime Policy**

**Applicable to**: This policy only applies to hourly workers. Salaried workers are not paid overtime, unless approved by Ops Leadership. 

Team members may volunteer to work overtime, but usually, overtime will be requested by the supervisor when there is a business need. Regardless of who requests overtime, overtime hours **MUST** be approved by the Ops Leadership. The overtime should never result in a shift over 12hrs, and the Ops Supervisor should adjust the break schedule according to the **“Meal and Break Policy.”**

Overtime hours are paid at 1.5x of a regular hourly rate. Overtime is defined as hours worked over 40hrs in a week. Therefore, if you missed hours on a certain day and worked hours over your schedule in a different day, those hours may not be calculated as overtime. They will first be allocated to compensate for hours missed, and only hours worked over a total of 40hrs in a week will be paid as overtime.

Any overtime hours worked on a holiday are paid at the regular overtime rate of 1.5x a regular hourly rate.

**Process documentation**

**How to request to work overtime (associates) →** Reach out to Operations Support Specialists or manager with the request

**How to submit Approved Overtime (associates) →** Once your overtime request was approved, your manager will update your shift in Deputy to reflect that. Failure to report approved overtime may result in not being paid for that time.

**Guidelines for Over Time**

-   Associates can grab OT posted if are over 60 days with Latchel.
    
-   No OT for Associates with less than 30 days with Latchel.
    
-   Associates between 30 and 60 days with Latchel, might need to ask for Supervisor approval.
    

## **Attendance Policy**

**Applicable to**: Hourly & salaried workers working on day-to-day operations

Latchel expects all team members to report to work on time for every scheduled shift. A team member who is unable to report to work at the designated time is required to notify his or her direct manager/supervisor as soon as practicable but no later than their scheduled start time, in accordance with attendance policies outlined above. Team members who fail to report to work (within a calendar month) for three (3) consecutive or four (4) non-consecutive business days, without notifying the company of the absence, will be considered as having voluntarily resigned as a result of job abandonment.

  
If the team member is unable to contact the company for any absence, he or she should ask a representative (such as a family member or friend) to do so on their behalf. If the team member or a representative is unable to contact Latchel due to extreme circumstances (such as a medical emergency or natural disaster that prohibits the team member or his or her representative from contacting the company within three (3) days), the team member or his or her representative must contact Latchel as soon as practicable to explain the situation. In extreme circumstances, Latchel may consider the explanation and its timing before determining if the voluntary resignation will be upheld.

**Team members who resigned as a result of job abandonment are not eligible for rehire.**

## **Late Arrivals Policy**

Latchel expects all team members to report to work on time for every scheduled shift. A team member who is unable to report to work at the designated time is required to notify his or her direct manager/supervisor as soon as practicable, but no later than their scheduled start time, in accordance with attendance policies outlined above.

Team members who fail to log in on time to work on three (3) occasions, within a two-week pay period, without notifying their supervisor before the start time, could be considered for contract termination.

Or, team members who fail to log in on time to work on four (4) occasions, within a calendar month, without notifying their supervisor before the start time, could be considered for contract termination.

## **Maternity/Paternity/Adoption Leave Policy**

**Applicable to:** Hourly and Salaried Workers

Welcoming a baby to the family is an important and exciting time and you should prepare for it. 

1.  **Unpaid Parental Leave:** International contractors are eligible for up to 4 weeks of unpaid leave with job protection. During this period, their position within the company will remain secure.
    
2.  **Usage of Paid Time Off (PTO):** Contractors may choose to use any accrued Paid Time Off to maintain income during their parental leave.
    
3.  **Bonus PTO for Long-Term Contractors:** Contractors with 2 or more years of service are awarded an additional one week of Paid Time Off as a bonus. This bonus PTO may be used during their parental leave or at another time of choice.
    

Please notify and discuss your need to take leave with your supervisor and People Operations thirty days before when you expect to be out. If that time frame is not possible, please notify them as soon as you are able.  
**How to submit Planned Time Off →** Go to Deputy > Click on your profile, located in the top right corner > Select **“Profile”**\> Leave > Add New. You will be able to see your PTO balance. You will be able to select either Paid Time Off (PTO) or Unpaid Time Off (UPTO, reflected as “Unpaid Leave” in Deputy) 

Note: This policy is designed to support our contractors during the adjustment period of parenthood, respecting and valuing the personal time needed in this important life moment. The company reserves the right to modify this policy as needed.

Please reach out to People Operations with any questions or concerns.
