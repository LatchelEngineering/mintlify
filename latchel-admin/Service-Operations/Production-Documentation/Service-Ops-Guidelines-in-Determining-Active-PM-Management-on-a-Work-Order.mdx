## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Info>
  We want to prevent confusion, double-work, and missed responsibilities on work orders where it isn’t clear who is in control. The goal is to help associates quickly identify when a PM is actively managing a work order (including “do not touch” scenarios) so they know when to proceed with the next action versus when to move the WO to Waiting for PM and hold off on further intervention.
</Info>

<Note>
  **🧠 Why This Matters:**<br />For emergency work orders, intentional delays and active coordination are incompatible. If a Property Manager (PM) is managing a work order, Latchel is no longer in control of the timeline. To prevent these jobs from going "stale" and to protect our SLA, we must transfer ownership using the **Forward to PM** tool. We do not leave emergencies in active statuses (like Scheduling or Pending) if the PM is the one doing the work.
</Note>

## 📋 PM Active Management Scenarios

<Note>
_These guidelines apply to all work orders any time we need to decide whether the PM is actively managing the work order. **However, for Emergencies, identifying active PM management must result in an immediate status change to 'Waiting for PM' (via the Forward to PM tool) to stop the SLA clock.** We do not allow emergencies to remain in active statuses like Pending or Scheduling once PM management is confirmed._

_The only exception is work orders that were moved to **Needs Approval** due to initial intake rules. Even if these work orders are later escalated, we do **not** consider them to be under active PM management, since **Needs Approval** is the only status they have held as a result of the initial intake process._
</Note>

<Note>
  **General Rule: Recent PM Activity**

  - **Non-Emergencies (48-Hour Rule):** If the PM has updated the work order in the past 48 hours (e.g., added a note, sent an email, or changed a status), treat it as actively managed. You may leave the work order in its current status and set a follow-up task for the PM.
  - **🛑 Emergency Rule (Immediate Action):** The 48-hour window does **not** apply to emergencies. If there is _any_ evidence of PM activity from today or yesterday that indicates they are taking control, you must act immediately:
    1. **Identify the Activity:** This includes the PM adding coordination notes, communicating directly with a resident/vendor, or assigning their own Service Provider.
    2. **The "Active Path" Test:** Ask yourself: _"Is Latchel still the primary coordinator?"_ If the PM's activity has paused our progress or changed the timeline (e.g., they told the resident they are 'looking into it'), we are no longer on an active emergency path.
    3. **Stop the Clock:** You must **Forward to PM** immediately.

  **Why:** Intentional delays (like a PM "reviewing" an emergency) and active emergency handling are incompatible. Leaving an emergency in an active status while a PM is "active" in the background creates a **Bad Status** (stale job). You must transfer ownership to stop the SLA clock and protect the team.
</Note>

## ✅ Next Steps After Determining Active PM Management

Once you have determined that a PM is actively managing a work order, your next step depends entirely on the **Priority** of the job.

### **🛑 The Emergency Rule (Path A Only)**

If the work order is an **Emergency**, you do not have the option to leave it in its current status. You must always follow **Path A** to stop the SLA clock:

1. **Use the "Forward to PM" tool immediately.** This transfers ownership to the PM and moves the job to "Waiting for PM" status.
2. **Reclassify to Urgent:** Unless the issue involves active Property Damage or Extreme Habitability, reclassify the severity to **Urgent**.
3. **Keep "(EMERGENCY)" in the Title:** When reclassifying to Urgent, you **must retain "(EMERGENCY)" in the work order title.** This ensures the Service Provider still treats the job with the necessary urgency.
4. **Fallback (No Tool Access):** If you do not have the "Forward to PM" tool, you must **immediately escalate to Support** to have the status moved manually.

   - _Why:_ Leaving an emergency in an active status (Scheduling/Pending) while a PM is managing it creates a **Bad Status** (stale job) that Latchel is unfairly held responsible for.

### **Path B — Non-Emergencies Only**

Use this path **only for Non-Emergencies** when the PM is managing the matter but has not explicitly asked Latchel to stop all action.

1. **Leave the WO in its current status.**
2. **Create a Task:** Assign a task to the **PM User Group** titled: _"Action Required: PM Instructions or Information Needed."_
3. **Set Automation:** Set the task to **"Complete task on any job state change."**

**Use the guidelines below to identify active PM management (examples include, but are not limited to):**

1. **Direct Communication:** A call, email, or message indicates the PM will handle the request.
   - _Sample:_ Resident says: _"My PM called me this morning and said they’re sending their own plumber today."_
2. **Coordination Notes:** A note in the WO (from the PM, a team member, or reported by the resident/SP) states the PM is taking ownership.
   - _Sample:_ PM note: _"We’ve assigned this to ABC Plumbing. They are scheduled for Thursday 3 PM."_
3. **External Handling:** The PM is handling the issue outside of Latchel (e.g., using a preferred vendor not in our system).
4. **Investigation Requests:** The PM explicitly asks Latchel to refrain from action while they investigate.
5. **Task Creation:** The PM creates a task for themselves or the PM group (e.g., _"PM to ask owner for approval"_).

### **Exceptions to the 48-Hour Rule**

Even if there has been no update in the last 48 hours, the team should not resume action and should treat the work order as PM‑managed when:

1. **Standing Instructions:** The PM has clearly stated in the WO summary, notes, or pre-dispatch instructions that they will be managing the work order moving forward.
2. **Active Coordination:** The PM has noted in the WO or created a task indicating they are personally coordinating approvals or next steps (e.g., working directly with the owner, resident, or service provider).
3. **Investigation:** PM requested no further Latchel action while they investigate or review options.
4. **Third-Party Coordination:** PM is coordinating directly with a warranty company or third party and will update once they hear back.
