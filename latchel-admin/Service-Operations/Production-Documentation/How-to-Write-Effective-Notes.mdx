## 🧠 Why This Matters

We want to prevent confusion, missed details, and delays caused by unclear or incomplete documentation in work orders and customer interactions. The goal is for every agent to write clear, comprehensive, and well-organized notes that communicate past, present, and future actions.

It's like passing the baton in a relay race. When a colleague hands you a Work Order, do you want them to leave out details and make the handoff sloppy — or set you up with everything you need to keep moving? The same goes when you're the one handing it off next.

Completing Work Order responsibilities isn't a race to the finish; it's a team effort. An effective note informs (1) other agents in Operations, (2) other teams at Latchel, and (3) Property Managers about your actions and the important details of the customer's situation.

### What are "Notes"?

This document mostly refers to the published Notes found on the Existing Work Order Interface — see: [(TECH 101) How to: Navigate the Existing Work Order Interface](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/440074480)

It's Latchel practice to always communicate effectively through the written text seen by customers, Operations agents, or other Latchel team members, for the sake of effective handling of each Work Order.

<Note>
  Working through a list of residents or escalation contacts? Each contact attempt gets its own note explaining why you reached out and what happened — the second and third calls need the same level of detail as the first.
</Note>

| PAST | PRESENT | FUTURE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| - What's broken — part, issue type, location in the home<br />- What earlier agents already tried (link the note, don't re-type it) | - What you did this session, in order<br />- How many follow-ups, and with whom<br />- One note per session, not one per micro-action | - What happens next, and roughly when<br />- Any task, forward, or escalation you created |

_This is one example of the pattern — unresponsive contacts are where notes tend to go vague fastest. But it's not the only place; the table further down covers plenty of moments that go smoothly and still get under-documented._

| Scenario | Skip this | Write this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resident unresponsive (troubleshooting) | _"Called resident 3x, reached voicemail."_ | _"Called resident 3x to troubleshoot the reported leak. Resident unresponsive to all 3 attempts. Forwarding the work order to the PM due to unresponsiveness."_ |

_The difference isn't just detail — the skip column doesn't say why the call was made or what happens next, and it implies waiting for a callback. The write column says why you called and what you actually did about it. You don't need to cite the SOP or spell out every rule you followed — just the outcome and the next concrete step._

## 🔒 Always Include

These apply to every note, on every work order, regardless of channel.

- If there are coordinating Assembly Ticket #s, always include them: Ticket (###) (action taken) (service provider) / (PM) / (resident). Example: "Ticket 1234 called (service provider)" or "Ticket 4321 email from (resident)." (This applies to SMS/Webchat/Email, which route through Assembly. A phone call doesn't generate a ticket, so don't invent one — and since the note lives directly on the work order you're calling about, you don't need to reference that WO# either.)
- If a caller is calling about an existing work order, note the caller's phone number on the WO. (This one's conditional — it applies to that specific scenario, not to every note.)
- If there are coordinating Work Order #s — a different, related WO, like a duplicate or a resumed job — always include that WO#. You don't need to reference the number of the WO you're already writing the note on.
- Summarize, in your own words, any conversation you had via SMS or Webchat with the customer.
- This applies to every call, email, or SMS on a work order — not just the first one. If you're working through a list of residents or escalation contacts, each contact attempt gets its own note explaining why you called and what happened.

<Warning>
  **No Abbreviations**

  No abbreviations should be used in speech (written or spoken) to external customers or internally with other Latchel teams. Special note to avoid abbreviating "follow-up," "emergency services," and "non-emergency services."
</Warning>

<Danger>
  **Never Include Credit Card Information**

  Do NOT put ANY raw credit card details into a note, task, or any other field/form. PMs have full access to the job log and can see every entry we create. For their security and ours, keep card details out of the log.
</Danger>

<Note>
  **Emergency Services & PM Visibility**

  These guidelines apply to all Emergency Services maintenance requests as well. When performing Emergency Services, remember the Property Manager can see status updates live — write notes clear enough that a PM watching in real time can follow what's happening.
</Note>

## 📋 Step-by-Step: What to Write

### Step 1 — Past Actions

1. Enter the Existing Work Order Interface and navigate to the Notes section.
2. Answer: what is broken or defective in the property?
   - Include the part that's broken (toilet, door, refrigerator, etc.)
   - Clarify the type of issue (leaking, not working, overheating, etc.)
   - Clarify where in the house the issue is located (living room, master bathroom, garage, etc.)

### Step 2 — Present Actions

1. Summarize what you've done so far — troubleshooting, asking the resident for availability, assigning a service provider, following up with the resident or service provider to schedule, etc.
   - Include how many follow-up calls were made, and who each one was for.
   - Update notes as actions happen rather than batching them, so nothing gets lost or altered. If a sequence of steps happens in one session, one summary note is fine.
2. If you're assigned a task that other agents already tried without success, escalate immediately.

### Step 3 — Future Actions

1. Answer: what will we do next?
   - If you can't complete an action right now, explain what you did instead — the task you created, the forward, the escalation — and when it's planned to happen.
2. As in Step 2: if the assigned task was already tried by another agent and didn't work, escalate immediately.

### Step 4 — Save the Note

1. Click "Save Note" to finish.

### Everyday moments — where the note still falls short

_Unresponsiveness isn't the only place notes go thin. Calls that connect, estimates that come in, payments that get issued — all of it is just as easy to under-document. Grounded in the actual scheduling, follow-up, estimate-handling, and payment SOPs._

| Scenario | Skip this | Write this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resident — troubleshooting call | _"Troubleshot with resident, couldn't fix it."_ | _"Called resident to troubleshoot no hot water at kitchen sink. Confirmed water heater pilot light is out; walked resident through the pilot relight steps per the troubleshooting video, unsuccessful. Proceeding with SP dispatch for pilot assembly repair."_ |
| Service Provider — follow-up on materials ETA | _"Talked to SP, no update yet."_ | _"Called assigned SP (ABC Plumbing) to check on materials ETA for the approved repair. SP confirmed materials still on order, no firm date. Not PD/EH — moved to Urgent."_ |
| Resident — confirming availability | _"Confirmed with resident."_ | _"Called resident to confirm availability. Resident confirmed available Fri 9/19, 8am–12pm. Called SP with that window — confirmed — and set the appointment for Fri 9/19."_ |
| Resident — SMS status update | _"Resident said leak stopped."_ | _"Ticket 5191 — SMS with resident. Resident confirmed leak has slowed after placing a bucket under the pipe; not urgent overnight. Advised SP is still scheduled to come out Thursday as planned."_ |
| Resident — SMS reschedule request | _"Resident asked to reschedule."_ | _"Ticket 6031 — resident texted asking to reschedule tomorrow's appointment; said they're available Friday instead. Called SP to confirm Friday works — confirmed. Updated appointment date in the WO."_ |
| Service Provider — estimate submitted via Assembly | _"Uploaded estimate."_ | _"Ticket 6027 — SP submitted a \$340 estimate for gutter repair via Assembly. Uploaded to WO Files; amount is within approved budget, so proceeding with scheduling — no PM approval needed."_ |
| Service Provider — payment issued (card) | _"Paid SP."_ | _"Service Provider confirmed job complete — issued card for \$220 (approved budget) covering the service visit and parts."_ |

_Every write-this example follows the same shape: why the contact or action happened, what was learned or decided, and what happens next. Swap in your own ticket numbers and names — the structure is what matters, not these exact words._

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

Do not break or deviate from this SOP on your own authority. Always escalate to an Ops Support specialist or follow explicit instructions if you believe an exception is necessary.
